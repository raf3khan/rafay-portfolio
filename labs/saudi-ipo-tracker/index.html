How the estimate works

Every valid subscriber first receives the minimum, usually 10 shares. The shares left after that are split in proportion to what each person applied for above the minimum. If the minimums alone would use up the whole tranche, the minimum isn't guaranteed and shares are spread evenly instead. Real allocations round down to whole shares and can follow a different rule set out in the prospectus, so treat this as a guide and check the final allocation notice from your receiving bank.

How this tracker works

Stages. A Saudi IPO runs in four steps. Institutions bid first during book-building, which sets the final price. Individuals then subscribe through their bank or broker during the retail window, usually two or three days, paying the full amount up front. Shares are allocated a few days later and the unused money is refunded. The stock then lists on Tadawul's Main Market, or on Nomu for smaller companies, where only qualified investors can subscribe.

ETFs and sukuk. The Saudi Exchange list also carries new exchange-traded funds and sukuk. A new ETF stays open for weeks rather than days and is subscribed through its fund manager, often by swapping shares you already hold for fund units. Units are issued at the offer price, so the allocation estimator doesn't apply. Sukuk are Shariah-compliant bonds, sold in certificates with a set face value, SAR 1,000 each for the WSM sukuk on the list now.

Allocation. Retail tranches are almost always oversubscribed. Each subscriber first gets a minimum, often 10 shares, and the rest is shared in proportion to what each person applied for. That's why a large subscription can come back mostly as a refund. Try different coverage and subscriber counts in the estimator above to see the range.

Where to subscribe. Individuals subscribe through a "receiving entity", which is a bank or a CMA-licensed broker named in the offering announcement. You need an investment account with one of them and enough cash in it for the full amount. Each IPO card lists its receiving entities once they're announced. Most large Saudi brokers usually take part, including Al Rajhi Capital, SNB Capital, Riyad Capital, Alinma Capital, Albilad Capital, AlJazira Capital, ANB Capital, SAB Invest, BSF Capital, Alistithmar Capital, Derayah Financial and Sahm Capital.

Where the dates come from. Each IPO is entered by hand from the Saudi Exchange IPO list and the offering announcements, so this can lag by a day or two. Stages update on their own from the dates.

Not investment advice. Always read the prospectus and confirm dates with your broker or receiving bank before you subscribe.
