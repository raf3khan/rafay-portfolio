How the two methods work

Avalanche: highest rate first

Pay every minimum, then put all your extra money on the debt with the highest APR. When it's gone, its payment rolls onto the next highest rate. This always costs the least interest.

Snowball: smallest balance first

Pay every minimum, then put all your extra money on the smallest balance. You clear whole debts sooner, which many people find easier to stick with, though it can cost more interest.

Your monthly total stays fixed

In both methods you keep paying the same total each month. When a debt is cleared, its minimum joins the extra payment on the next target, which is why progress speeds up over time.

Use the APR, not the flat rate

Many personal finance and car finance contracts quote a flat profit rate, which looks lower than the true annual rate. Use the APR from your contract. As a rough guide, a flat rate over several years is close to half the APR.

Plain maths, no lender's rules. Interest is charged monthly at APR ÷ 12 on what you still owe. Minimum payments are held fixed until a debt is paid off. Real contracts can differ: some fixed-profit contracts don't reduce the profit when you pay early, some charge early settlement fees, and card minimums usually shrink as the balance falls.
