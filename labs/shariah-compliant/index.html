How the halal stock screener works

The screener follows AAOIFI Shariah Standard No. 21 (Financial Papers), the rulebook most Islamic banks and Shariah boards in Saudi Arabia and the Gulf use for shares. A stock passes when its main business is permissible and three ratios stay under their limits: interest-bearing debt under 30% of market value, interest-bearing cash and securities under 30% of market value, and non-permissible income under 5% of total revenue. Other screens, such as some index providers, divide by total assets or use a 33% limit, so their results can differ.

Pure and mixed. A compliant stock is "pure" when the company has no interest-bearing debt or deposits and no non-permissible income at all. Most compliant stocks are "mixed": their main business is permissible, they stay under every limit, and the small non-permissible share is purified.

Where the US data comes from

Balance sheet and income figures come from each company's latest 10-K and 10-Q filings in SEC EDGAR, and are refreshed every weekday after the US market closes. The date of each figure is shown next to the result. Business type comes from the company's SEC industry code, with a few hand-checked corrections. Prices are the latest daily close. All cash is treated as interest-bearing, because filings rarely separate Islamic deposits, so the cash check errs on the strict side. When a filing doesn't show interest income or debt clearly, the check is marked for review instead of guessed. Tadawul stocks are checked from figures you enter from the annual report.

How purification works

Even a compliant company earns a little interest. Scholars ask investors to give their share of that income to charity each year. The calculator divides the company's non-permissible income by its shares outstanding and multiplies by the shares you hold.

This is screening, not a fatwa. It is an educational tool that applies published rules to public figures, which can be out of date or misread from filings. Ask a qualified scholar before you invest.
