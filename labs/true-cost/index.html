How the BNPL maths works. Each instalment is price ÷ number of payments. The first is paid at checkout and the rest on schedule. A missed payment adds the late fee and is paid the number of days late you set. Fees stop at the cap if your plan has one.

The headline rate looks only at the late payment: late fee ÷ instalment × 365 ÷ days late. That is what the fee charges you for those few extra days.

The whole-plan APR treats the purchase as a loan and finds the yearly rate that makes what you paid, on the days you paid it, equal to the price. It is the same idea as the APR quoted on a loan. Without late fees it is 0%. The cost is entirely in the slips.

How the card maths works. Each month, interest is balance × APR ÷ 12, added before you pay. The minimum is the larger of the % of balance and the floor, so it shrinks as the balance shrinks. That is why minimum-only payoff drags on for years.

Islamic cards. If your card charges a fixed monthly fee instead of interest, turn it into a rate with monthly fee ÷ balance × 12 × 100 and enter that as the APR.

This assumes no new spending, no late fees and no rate changes. Any of those make it worse.
